What the data says

  1. repair volume grew 6.8% from 2023 to 2025 (9,286 to 9,915 bad orders)
  2. winter peak is real: Dec runs 17% above average and Jun runs 13% below, a 34% swing between peak and trough
  3. BOXCAR is the problem fleet at 1.011 bad orders per car per year, 79% above AUTORACK
  4. cars past 40 years fail 90% more often than cars under 10, and the curve is flat until about 20 years then bends up
  5. 9 of 14 defect codes cause 80% of all bad orders, led by wheel set thin flange
  6. sill or frame cracked is only 2.88% of bad orders but 13.37% of repair spend, a 4.64x ratio, so a volume-ranked report would underrate it
  7. shop turnaround is uneven after adjusting for repair mix: Waycross holds cars 24% longer than the network and Kansas City 14% shorter, which is a scheduling problem, not a mix problem
  8. 515 cars (4.3% of the fleet) had 6 or more bad orders in 3 years and account for 11.7% of repair spend

Six candidate models, all scored on the same six months that none of them were trained on. The winner is refit on the full history and used to project the next half year. Intervals come from how wrong the model actually was on the holdout, not from a formula that assumes the model is right.

The backtest score is not the whole story. Gradient boosting scored marginally best on the holdout and is still the wrong choice. A tree can only predict values it has seen, so asked about 2026 it flattens out: it projects -0.4% growth against a history that has grown about 3% a year. The linear model with Fourier seasonality projects +3.4%. For a planning forecast that has to extrapolate, that matters more than a tenth of a point of MAPE.

Shop assignment plan for 2026-01

The forecast says 961 cars will be bad ordered in the peak month. This is where to send them. Cost per car is repair hours times the shop labor rate times a specialty penalty, plus deadhead miles. Constraints are shop capacity and a 1,500 mile cap on any single move. Compared against nearest shop in region with spillover, which is close to what the history shows happening today, where 14% of repairs went outside the home region.

READ THIS CAREFULLYThe optimizer does not spread work evenly, and it should not. It fills the shops with the best combination of rate and location until their capacity runs out, then works outward. Five of ten shops finish at exactly capacity, which says capacity is the binding constraint, so buying a slot at a cheap shop is worth more than negotiating a rate anywhere. It also drops one shop to zero cars, which is a question for management rather than an answer from a model.
